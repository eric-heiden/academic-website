Newton SPH Fluid Support

Upstream integration, opacity-aware ViewerGL rendering, correctness validation, and performance tuning for the experimental weakly-compressible SPH solver.

What changed

ViewerGL compatibility

Rebased the fluid surface and spray batches onto the current render orchestration, including opaque geometry, translucent OIT geometry, shadow/depth passes, layer activation, model clearing, and headless capture.

Solver correctness

Restored diffuse emission for isolated ballistic particles, which have no SPH neighbors but should qualify as surface spray. CPU and CUDA regression cases now cover the corrected slot-selection path.

Hot-path work

Skipped vorticity computation when confinement is disabled, refreshed anisotropy and diffuse render buffers every second step in the examples, and enabled CUDA graph capture by default with an explicit opt-out.

Public surface

Registered SolverSPH in Newton’s lazy public solver exports, marked it experimental in the solver guide, updated example discovery, and added Towncrier fragments.

Implementation

Simulation and rendering pipeline

The implementation is a weakly-compressible SPH solver with an optional position-based density projection. Calling the entire solver “XPBD” is imprecise: pressure, viscosity, cohesion, surface tension, vorticity, and XSPH terms remain SPH kernels, while the projection supplies the XPBD/PBF-style density correction.

→
5ViewerGLDepth, thickness, shade, OIT

Opacity integration

ViewerGL first separates opaque and translucent instance batches. Opaque objects populate the regular depth and shadow paths. Translucent geometry accumulates weighted color and revealage in the OIT targets. The fluid surface is reconstructed independently from particle depth and thickness, bilateral filtering, and screen-space shading; both results are composited without treating the water as an ordinary alpha-sorted mesh.

Interpretation

The decimation gain is repeatable across the sampled particle counts because render anisotropy and diffuse classification are visual derivatives, not state integration. Updating them at 30 Hz while simulation advances at 60 Hz removes work without changing the simulated particle state. These figures are single-host measurements, not cross-platform guarantees.

06

Scope

Known limitations

  • Experimental API. SolverSPH is intentionally marked experimental; parameter names and behavior can evolve before stabilization.
  • Rendering is screen-space. Surface quality depends on camera resolution, filter parameters, particle sampling, and thickness coverage. It is not a watertight geometry export.
  • Diffuse particles are visual. Foam, spray, and bubbles improve readability but do not feed momentum back into the simulated fluid.
  • Performance numbers are local. The reported solver benchmark excludes OpenGL rasterization and should be repeated on target hardware, especially when interop falls back to host transfer.
  • Coverage is finite. The examples exercise free surfaces, sustained forcing, rigid coupling, transparency, and headless capture; they do not establish production readiness for every scale or material regime.

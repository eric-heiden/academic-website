Abstract

The XPBD fluid work is integrated with Newton’s current collision, layer, opacity, fullscreen, and headless ViewerGL architecture. The integrated solver advances approximately 100,000 particles at a median 90.2 simulation frames per second on an RTX 4090. ViewerGL now reconstructs fluid surfaces at framebuffer resolution instead of half resolution, removing the visibly stepped silhouettes seen in the previous report. Six ten-second, 720p recordings below exercise powered pumping, a passive water wheel, free surfaces, transparent containment, multiple fluid materials, buoyant rigid bodies, foam, articulated manipulation, and sustained wave forcing.

1. Results

The branch is current with upstream main, the XPBD path is restored, and the rendering regression is corrected.

Current ViewerGL behavior is preserved

Fluid batches follow ViewerGL’s layer-qualified lifecycle and render after multisample resolve. Current weighted transparency, opacity grouping, fullscreen rendering, image capture, frame limits, shadow passes, and CUDA interop remain intact.

Fluid surfaces render at native framebuffer resolution

The previous half-size depth and thickness targets reconstructed a 720p image from a 640×360 fluid buffer. Native-resolution targets remove the blocky contour and thin-sheet aliasing without changing the simulation state.

Stability protections cover failure-prone boundaries

World-qualified neighbor queries prevent cross-world coupling; density corrections no longer cancel shape contacts; non-finite particle corrections self-heal before contaminating the hash grid; and configurable rigid-body motion limits contain force spikes.

Cold compilation carries less dead work

Obsolete duplicate fluid kernels were removed from the general XPBD module. The dedicated fluid module remains the sole implementation, reducing a clean CUDA compilation of the XPBD kernel module from 26.65 s to 24.67 s on this host.

2. ViewerGL evidence

Every recording is a direct headless ViewerGL capture from the integrated XPBD checkout: 1280×720, 30 fps, ten seconds, H.264, with native example materials.

3. Integrated simulation and rendering path

The solver and renderer share particle state but remain separate: visual smoothing and foam do not alter the physical solution.

  1. CollisionCurrent CollisionPipeline generates shape contacts.
  2. XPBD solveGrouped hash-grid density, cohesion, viscosity, and body reaction advance each substep.
  3. Render fitOptional smoothing and anisotropy derive ellipsoids from the solved state.
  4. Surface passesDepth, thickness, bilateral blur, shadows, and refraction reconstruct the liquid.
  5. CompositeFluid and diffuse foam join the resolved scene with correct depth and opacity.

4. Implementation

Fluid behavior is enabled through the existing XPBD solver; ViewerGL consumes render-only particle derivatives.

Listing 1. XPBD fluid configuration 
solver = newton.solvers.SolverXPBD(
    model,
    iterations=2,
    fluid_density=1000.0,
    fluid_cohesion=0.6,
    fluid_viscosity=0.03,
    fluid_max_neighbors=128,
    max_diffuse_particles=20_000,
)

collision = newton.CollisionPipeline(model)
contacts = collision.contacts()

for _ in range(substeps):
    state.clear_forces()
    collision.collide(state, contacts)
    solver.step(state, next_state, control, contacts, dt)
    state, next_state = next_state, state

Performance-oriented choices

  1. Spatial reordering. Morton sorting restores locality for repeated neighbor reads and is skipped for mixed topological particle systems.
  2. Bounded tail work. An optional neighbor cap limits rare over-compressed particles from stalling an entire GPU warp.
  3. Graph-safe storage. Group metadata, diffuse buffers, and reorder scratch are allocated before CUDA graph capture.
  4. Render fast paths. Unsmoothed output reuses solved positions, render decimation is optional, and the simulation hash grid is reused when safe.

7. Conclusion

The XPBD fluid branch is again based on current Newton, preserves the modern ViewerGL opacity and layer model, removes the half-resolution surface regression, and sustains a median 90.2 fps at roughly 100,000 particles on the measured GPU. Remaining limits are explicit: screen-space rendering is view dependent, diffuse particles are visual, and the robot capture’s IK workload is not representative of solver throughput.

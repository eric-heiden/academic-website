SHAC with MuJoCo Warp

Latest current-branch refresh, June 18 2026 Pacific time: the bad-looking Ant replay was traced to a report harness action-order regression, not a renderer overlay or a basic Newton/MJWarp dynamics failure. The Isaac-style DiffRL Ant policy used in the current PPO and SHAC audits was trained against Newton's joint-force slice order; sending those actions in MJCF actuator order pins the hips/ankles at the wrong limits and produces the stuck right-leaning gait. The runners now default that Isaac-style DiffRL Ant path to ant_action_order=joint, while still allowing explicit actuator-order probes for older diagnostics. A fresh d6 MJWarp audit of the PPO actor reaches 8.65 m over 480 MuJoCo-contact steps with 0/1024 falls and 0 invalid worlds across three no-reset repeats. A follow-up clone-count robustness audit showed why the earlier single-count SHAC selector was too optimistic: long-horizon Ant outcomes vary with vectorized world count after tiny contact-order perturbations. The current promoted SHAC actor is therefore selected by the worst score across 1, 16, 256, 512, and 1024 MuJoCo-contact worlds. It improves the PPO anchor's worst audited displacement from 7.89 m to 8.63 m, with 0 falls/invalids and minimum height/up of 0.561 m / 0.970 in the 1024-world replay. The corrected H=8 finite-difference check around the PPO actor now has about 4.1e-4 mean relative policy-gradient error with perfect sign agreement for MuJoCo, Newton, and no-contact stepping. The direct MuJoCo-contact SHAC failure was therefore mostly optimizer/selector scale in the harness: clipped Adam steps were far smaller than the useful flat-gradient step, and single-count selection could choose batch-shape luck. The robust line search accepts one full H=8 MuJoCo-gradient step from the PPO anchor, then a tiny signed corrective step; further fixed SGD steps are rejected by the worst-count guard. The faster Newton-gradient/MuJoCo-eval continuation still reaches 10.77 m, but with a lower height margin and a single-count selector, so it is retained as a speed diagnostic rather than the promoted raw-contact result. All promoted clips below are raw ViewerGL.get_frame() frames rendered with one shared smoothed COM follow camera and no overlays. The MJWarp branch used here includes the differentiability fixes from this investigation: free-joint kinematics replay, dense constrained mass-solve VJPs, explicit RNE qfrc_bias/cfrc_int adjoints, branch-level kinematics replay, contact-row aref/D/J adjoints, contact buffer isolation, control buffer isolation, and Newton MJCF per-DOF joint-anchor preservation.

What Is Fixed

The MJWarp gradient path now includes fixes for free-joint kinematics replay, dense mass-solve VJPs, RNE qfrc_bias/cfrc_int adjoints, in-place state output isolation in _advance, and repeated root writes in com_vel. This update adds active contact-row aref/D VJPs, contact-row position gradients for all pyramidal rows, contact buffer isolation, and the missing quadratic-row efc.J force-law term. The latest fix computes differentiable kinematics once per body level instead of replaying body_branches; this removes the duplicated root-pitch gradient in branched planar bodies and drops Cheetah no-contact state/action error to 6.4e-5. The newest cinert adjoint hook improves Ant H=2 policy finite differences from 3.5e-1 to 1.3e-1 and Cheetah/MuJoCo policy finite differences to 1.1e-2. The newest RNE fix replaces auto-backward through qfrc_bias with an explicit adjoint and disables auto-backward through the cfrc tree accumulation; Hopper no-limit smooth dynamics now has 2.7e-4 full state/action error and Ant H=5 one-step state/action error is 8.1e-5. On the harness side, Newton-contact vectorized environments now use nonzero world spacing, PPO checkpoints can warm-start SHAC actors, Ant actions can follow either IsaacLab-like joint order or MuJoCo actuator order, the Isaac-style Ant height reward now uses ant_height_weight, checkpoint selection can include forward displacement, initial PPO warm starts are evaluated and saved before any SHAC update, and SHAC selection can now run on a separate large randomized environment batch via --selection-num-envs. The PPO runner now mirrors that safeguard for loaded actors by evaluating and saving update 0 before any PPO fine-tune step. SHAC can also use a no-LayerNorm actor architecture that exactly matches PPO checkpoints. Shared policy evaluation now preserves explicit action semantics: SHAC actors output raw pre-tanh actions when configured with action_squash=tanh, while PPO actors with action_squash=none are evaluated without an accidental second tanh. SHAC can now regularize updates against a frozen reference policy using an optional action-space anchor penalty, which makes the new Ant speed probes reproducible rather than one-off checkpoint edits. Ant selection thresholds now use recorded minima such as min_up and min_height instead of batch means, so rare inverted worlds are penalized instead of hidden by high average uprightness. Training warmup rollouts now run under torch.no_grad() and detach their returned state, so late-state probes initialize the differentiable horizon without accidentally backpropagating through hundreds of warmup steps. Rejected guarded SHAC updates now also restore q, qd, previous action, and progress, not just actor, optimizer, critic, and normalizer state; this prevents a rejected proposal from silently moving the next differentiable horizon to a rejected rollout state. The newest Ant harness fix uses the stable -90 degree X free-root start quaternion for both DiffRL and NV Ant imports, because this is the pose that matches the configured start joints after Newton exports to the MuJoCo/MJWarp runtime. It also drops the embedded NV floor before adding Newton's ground plane; this removes the duplicate coplanar floor contacts, and --ant-armature now sets actuated hinge armature explicitly. The default for both DiffRL and NV/IsaacLab Ant is 0.05, matching IsaacLab's actuator config instead of preserving the NV XML's lower 0.01 value. The newest Ant runner fix clamps all policy actions before force assignment, so native no-squash PPO and SHAC now use the same bounded action contract as DiffRL and IsaacLab. A direct velocity probe also verified that the imported Ant root convention is linear velocity in qd[0:3] and angular velocity in qd[3:6]; the remaining Ant issue is not a swapped velocity observation. The newest reward-bookkeeping fix also computes the Isaac-style energy term in actuator order and changes the DOF-limit cost from an upper-limit-only check to abs(dof_pos_scaled) > 0.98, so crouched lower-limit gaits are penalized. The shared report renderer now follows a smoothed mass-weighted COM target and no longer resets videos on falls, so visible rollouts show the actual policy trajectory rather than a reset-spliced episode. Hopper stochastic resets are clamped inside finite joint limits. The newest Newton fix preserves MJCF joint anchor positions per expanded DOF; this fixes Hopper's root slide/hinge anchors when SolverMuJoCo exports merged Newton joints back to MuJoCo and when it refreshes the MJWarp runtime model. Every run records the imported Newton and MJWarp commits in its JSON artifact. The latest Hopper harness fix moves the deterministic start 0.05 rad inside the two upper joint limits; exact-limit starts had clean root/action gradients but bad joint-position VJPs and made SHAC checkpoint selection fragile.

PPO now works as an environment sanity check for all three locomotion tasks, and SHAC has clean stepping-stone results for Cheetah and Hopper. Ant is still not fully solved for high-speed SHAC: the fresh d6 PPO audit is reliable at 8.65 m over 480 steps, direct MuJoCo-contact SHAC now improves that to a strict 8.93 m with 0 falls/invalids, and the Newton-gradient/MuJoCo-eval continuation reaches 10.77 m but with lower height margin. Current Ant Diagnosis

The bad-looking Ant clip was not caused by overlays: the promoted videos are raw ViewerGL.get_frame() output. The reproducible bug was action order. The Isaac-style DiffRL PPO actor in this report was trained against Newton joint-force slices; when the runner defaulted to MJCF actuator order, the exact same checkpoint moved only 0.03 m over 480 steps, spent most of the rollout pressed into joint limits, and visually stuck with the pole-like right-leaning gait. Restoring joint-order action mapping gives 8.65 m over 480 MuJoCo-contact steps with 0 falls/invalids in three 1024-world no-reset repeats.

This separates the current issues. PPO is now a valid dynamics positive control for the Ant setup. Direct MuJoCo-contact SHAC from that PPO actor improves once the optimizer uses a step in the line-search scale, but the earlier 8.93 m single-count result was not robust to vectorized clone count. The current selector audits each candidate at 1, 16, 256, 512, and 1024 worlds and keeps the worst score. The failed fixed-lr continuation is now explained: after the first step, both positive fixed SHAC steps and most anti-gradient steps reduce the robust 480-step score, so continued Ant improvement needs line search or a better long-horizon objective. A Newton-gradient / MuJoCo-eval H=8 line search matches the MuJoCo-gradient line search at the reset PPO state, so the remaining Ant bottleneck is not a simple contact-backend gradient sign error there. The faster current SHAC result still uses Newton contacts for differentiable training and MuJoCo contacts for selection/evaluation; it reaches 10.77 m with 0 falls/invalids but lower height margin and without the robust count selector. Code Findings

The most important MJWarp fixes were not contact-specific. First, free-joint kinematics were recomputed once per branch, corrupting qpos gradients for floating roots. Second, dense mass assembly had no useful generated adjoint for the dense matrix path used by the solve. Third, RNE force accumulation needed explicit adjoints for cfrc_int, cinert, cdof, and related kinematic intermediates. Fourth, _advance had to isolate output qpos/qvel arrays under AD to avoid overwriting saved inputs. Fifth, Newton's free-joint force route writes to MJWarp xfrc_applied; the support.apply_ft map into qfrc_smooth needed an explicit VJP for free-root force controls. The latest Hopper free-fall drilldown showed the key cancellation bug: CPU MuJoCo and MJWarp both produce pose-independent free-fall acceleration, but AD still injected root-angle and joint-position gradients. Finite-difference decomposition showed the dense M VJP was correct; the residual came from leaving Warp auto-AD active through qfrc_bias = dot(cdof, cfrc) and the cfrc tree accumulation while also recording the custom RNE adjoint. The fix disables those auto-backward kernels and records an explicit qfrc_bias adjoint that routes cdof.grad and cfrc_total.grad exactly once. The latest Newton-side Hopper bug was an importer/exporter mismatch: MJCF can define multiple scalar root joints on one body with distinct joint pos anchors, while Newton merges them into one D6-style joint. SolverMuJoCo was exporting every expanded MuJoCo joint with the merged child transform position, and the runtime mjw_model.jnt_pos refresh repeated the same mistake. A new per-DOF mujoco:dof_jnt_pos attribute now preserves those anchors through import, export, and MJWarp data refresh.

SHAC Findings

The harness had its own bug: early terminal falls still bootstrapped from the critic. DiffRL sets the terminal value to zero for early termination. Matching that behavior, and making terminal fall reward a fixed negative value, removes the high-speed crash exploit. The new upright gate also rejects the rolled-over progress policy. The remaining failure is no longer a reset-state smooth-dynamics gradient bug: Hopper and Ant short-horizon state/action checks now pass, but SHAC training still trades progress against repeated contacts and active limits poorly. Ant H=8 fixed-action rollouts now isolate the gap to grazing foot contacts: local action derivatives are good at reset, then contact-position and orientation finite differences diverge once foot distances sit just inside the inclusion margin. Hopper with limits on is nonsmooth at reset because the thigh and leg joints start on their upper stops; reset noise is now clamped back inside finite limits, and the new inside-limit start-pose probe confirms this was a real harness bug. Earlier Gym-style reward and upright warm-start Hopper probes still became falling policies, but the inside-limit deterministic start plus 1024-world fall-gated selector gives a clean 13.65 m SHAC result. Cheetah remains the cleanest success case, and PPO verifies the faithful 16-substep Cheetah environment. Ant needed environment fixes beyond the MJWarp adjoints: local Isaac-style observations/reward terms, asset-specific starts, bounded action handling, and an explicit action-order option. The newest audit found that the current Isaac-style DiffRL Ant actor is a joint-order policy; evaluating it as an actuator-order policy was the source of the wrong pinned video. Under the current d6 branch, the PPO positive control reaches 8.65 m over 480 steps with 0 falls/invalids in three 1024-world no-reset repeats. Direct MuJoCo-contact SHAC now improves that actor to 8.93 m when the update uses guarded SGD at the H=8 line-search scale, while the faster Newton-gradient diagnostic reaches 10.77 m with lower height margin. The remaining blocker is posture/contact robustness under longer differentiable horizons. The newest SHAC instrumentation records selection minima and posture shortfalls in every history row. Progress-2 SHAC continuation did not beat the selected actor: safe updates stayed below 14.79 m, while updates that chased more progress first lost uprightness, with min-up falling to roughly 0.72-0.84; torso height was not the limiting constraint. For native NV Ant, the no-reset single deterministic videos now look physically continuous, but large identical-world replays expose robustness failures because contact dynamics amplify tiny per-world floating-point differences by mid-rollout. The 500k-penalty NV SHAC continuation reduced those failures but still ended at 14 falls/3 invalids, so NV remains a diagnostic asset rather than the headline result. The remaining problem is qualitative and algorithmic: Hopper SHAC needed the start-pose active-set fix and a much stronger fall gate before it became reliable, while Ant SHAC now needs a long-horizon update that improves the bounded-action gait without exploiting a fragile contact branch. Contact finite differences still degrade once Ant feet sit on grazing active-set boundaries, but the strict scaled actor also shows that local derivatives can be sign-consistent while candidate updates remain bad for 960-step reliability. Adam was not the whole explanation: guarded SGD probes also rolled back every H=16/H=32 candidate, and removing anchor/survival shaping or freezing the actor backbone did not produce an accepted hard-contact update. The current no-reset matrix is Cheetah SHAC at 10.41 m, Hopper SHAC at 13.65 m, and Ant SHAC at 10.77 m from the Newton-gradient/MuJoCo-eval continuation. The open Ant target is still a faster zero-terminal SHAC gait under the same joint-order, bounded-action contract. It no longer looks like a single RNE mass/bias cancellation bug.

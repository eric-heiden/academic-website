Bottom line

The learner failed first

MJWarp and CPU MuJoCo closely replay the same failing actions, and analytic gradients of the complete configured objective agree with finite differences on nominal and pre-fall states. The reproduced collapse instead coincides with a nearly constant saturated critic plus a myopic, over-scaled actor update. A corrective bundle addressing these and other harness choices produces Ant locomotion and substantially delays Humanoid's fall. The experiment does not ablate each change, does not solve Humanoid, and does not erase the PR's topology-sensitive contact-gradient limitation.

01 · ViewerGL rollouts

Watch the failure—and the corrected policies

These are real checkpoint rollouts from the exact MJWarp PR, rendered headlessly at 960×540 and 50 fps by Newton's ViewerGL.

What ViewerGL is showing

The renderer advances one fixed, noise-free lane through MJWarpTorchBridge._forward_raw, records every qpos, and freezes after the first terminal transition. Native MuJoCo performs forward kinematics only to convert those recorded joint states into body poses; it never advances dynamics. ViewerGL then draws the corresponding Newton model on CPU with CUDA interop disabled. Each linked manifest hashes the checkpoint, policy state, trajectory, renderer, poster, and H.264/yuv420p MP4.

02 · Failure diagnosis

Three diagnosed learner-side contributors

The diagnosis uses the unchanged v1 checkpoints. It separates a simulator/adjoint problem from an optimization-system problem before changing the trainer.

01 / SCALE

The actor loss omitted ÷ horizon

The original harness averaged worlds but not time. Against the released SHAC update, gradients were H-times larger before clipping: 8× for Ant and 4× for Humanoid.

Ant clipped in 39–58 of 100 updates by seed (maximum pre-clip norm 48.26); Humanoid clipped in 91–94.
02 / CRITIC

The tanh critic became nearly constant

The terminal value supplied most of the numerical actor objective, but the second tanh layer saturated, removing the state derivative needed to see beyond the short rollout.

99.976–100% of final-policy second-layer activations have |h|>.99. Humanoid's final target ‖dV/dobs‖₂ is exactly zero in all three seeds.
03 / MYOPIA

Twenty milliseconds rewards a dive

Humanoid's H4 window ends at 0.020 s; its final-policy fall occurs at 0.215 s. At the nominal pose, alive, upright, height, and zero-control terms have no useful first-order stability signal, leaving forward acceleration.

The terminal critic contributes about 85% of the final-policy objective while its gradient is flat. Final action RMS reaches .557–.565, versus .00328–.00361 for the selected epoch-0 policies.

06 · API and method contract

Direct MJWarp bridge, inside Newton's environment

This distinction is important: the experiment uses Newton's current package stack for integration, but does not claim gradients through SolverMuJoCo.

enable_grad()

Called before model upload or differentiated compilation.

step(m, dᵢ, dₒ)

The PR's out-of-place form records one analytic adjoint.

Warp Tape VJP

Output qpos/qvel cotangents seed the reverse pass.

PyTorch autograd

The bridge returns qpos, qvel, and control gradients.

At Newton commit d37f4d3, the adapter still invokes the in-place two-argument step. PR #1535 records its analytic backward only for the out-of-place API after explicit opt-in.

Corrected SHAC ingredients

  • Reparameterized stochastic training actor and deterministic evaluation.
  • Differentiable terminal value from a frozen target critic.
  • Online critic fit to TD(λ), λ=0.95 targets.
  • Polyak target update, actor/critic Adam β=(0.7, 0.95), and linear LR decay.
  • Physical state continues across windows until a health or episode reset; graph history stops at each boundary.

Why the label is “SHAC-style”

  • The bridge carries only qpos/qvel; qacc warm-start is fixed and detached each step.
  • The compact trainer and qpos/qvel observation are not the full released training system.
  • Masks and resets are nondifferentiable boundaries; models require stateless actuators.
  • Tasks, rewards, time steps, and evaluation differ from the original DFlex benchmarks.
  • Ant uses H8 and the legacy report reward; Humanoid uses a smaller training budget and reduced observation.

07 · Limits and artifacts

Everything needed to inspect or rerun it

The result JSONs include full CLI configuration, timestamps, package and device versions, model/source hashes, every training update, and evaluation histories.

Interpretation limits

  • n=3 seeds and 200 Ant / 150 Humanoid updates are a corrective test, not convergence.
  • No PPO or model-free baseline is used; scores should not be compared with the SHAC paper.
  • Central differences are sampled directional tests, not a proof of the full Jacobian.
  • Contact topology and closest-feature selection are nonsmooth; equal counts are insufficient.
  • The full-objective parameter check covers the v1 H8/H4 failures, not every state in the corrected H32×3 Humanoid run.
  • Humanoid v1 and v2 raw returns are not comparable because the corrective run changes reward and reset distribution.
  • PR #1535 is still a draft and may change after the exact head captured here.
  • The tested bridge supports only stateless motor actuators; this report does not exercise equality constraints, tendons, flex, fluid forces, or other actuator types.

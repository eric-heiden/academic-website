SHAC with MuJoCo Warp

Newton experiments coupling SHAC-style differentiable policy optimization to the MuJoCo Warp differentiability branch. The gradient pass is now substantially fixed for Ant one-step and two-step diagnostics, and Ant can train to a reliable upright policy. Reliable forward locomotion is still not solved: higher progress reward creates movement, but it also reintroduces falls or invalid resets.

What Is Fixed

The MJWarp gradient path now includes fixes for free-joint kinematics replay, dense mass-solve VJPs, RNE qfrc_bias/cfrc_int adjoints, in-place state output isolation in _advance, and repeated root writes in com_vel. With implicit Euler damping disabled for this experiment, Ant no-contact and Newton-contact state Jacobians match finite differences near the expected finite-difference noise floor.

What Is Not Fixed

A reliable upright Ant policy trains, but reliable locomotion does not. The original reward/selection let a high-speed fallen policy win. Setting fall reward to a fixed terminal penalty and zeroing terminal critic bootstrap removes that exploit. Increasing forward-progress weight gives real movement, but the best moving policy still falls during the 8-second eval. More work is needed in either long-horizon/contact gradients or the SHAC task formulation.

Code Findings

The most important MJWarp fixes were not contact-specific. First, free-joint kinematics were recomputed once per branch, corrupting qpos gradients for floating roots. Second, dense mass assembly had no useful generated adjoint for the dense matrix path used by the solve. Third, RNE force accumulation needed explicit adjoints for cfrc_int, cinert, cdof, and related kinematic intermediates. Fourth, _advance had to isolate output qpos/qvel arrays under AD to avoid overwriting saved inputs.

SHAC Findings

The harness had its own bug: early terminal falls still bootstrapped from the critic. DiffRL sets the terminal value to zero for early termination. Matching that behavior, and making terminal fall reward a fixed negative value, removes the high-speed crash exploit. The remaining failure is not a simple one-step gradient sign bug; it appears when the task tries to trade long-horizon progress against contact-rich stability.
